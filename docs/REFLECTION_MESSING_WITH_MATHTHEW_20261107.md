## The layers, from the ground up

**1. The verse.** ὁ δὲ τὸ ἓν λαβὼν ἀπελθὼν ὤρυξεν γῆν καὶ ἔκρυψεν τὸ ἀργύριον τοῦ κυρίου αὐτοῦ. A man is given one token, goes, digs, and hides it. The parable is about value that was buried instead of used.

**2. The manual decode (GGSD).** Each word is opened to its lexical range instead of collapsed to one gloss. ὤρυξεν becomes four acts at once: reveal, conceal, channel, perforate. κυρίου is opened to its adjective sense (lawful, valid, bounded). αὐτοῦ becomes a location, and the location is the reader. The period becomes `.`, the current directory. The verse ends where you are standing.

**3. "Now lies."** This is the hinge, and it is a pun that works in both directions at once. The token *now lies* in the ground. And, from this word on, *now lies*: the untruth begins. "Still lies" carries both too: the coin is still buried, and the lying is still going on. The digging is the opening of the lie, which is why he heard ὤρυξεν "open."

**4. The token and the authority.** ἀργύριον is a token: it is worth something only because an authority says so (κυρίου, yoga-sai-tal, "the authority that grants the token value"). He then points the same sentence at the title. *Kata Maththew* is also a token, an expression whose value was set by an authority, and whose meaning that authority changed. The verse about a buried token is itself a buried token. The parable and its own transmission have the same shape.

**5. Turning error to its place.** ἔκρυψεν = `ril-fal-hal`, turn-err-place, covered by his favourite LSJ sense (Arist. *Top.* 156a7: argue so the opponent is unwarily led to an adverse conclusion). The servant does it in the parable: his defence in vv. 24–25 places the fault at the master's feet. An authority that re-means a text to escape what the text says about it does the same thing. One verb names both.

**6. The question marks.** "Sent?" and "Just that?" are his additions. They mark where the voice changes hands, from Matthew to the iguana reading Matthew. The question mark is the seam of authorship, left visible on purpose.

**7. "No! There."** αὐτοῦ also means *there*, *in that very place*. So the last word of the verse is a pointing word, and he lets it point outward: at his own decode (not a true decoding, though it names the lying) and at expression itself (representation, which may be lies). "Exactly there! The expression. That." The verse's final deixis is turned on the page you are reading.

**8. The period as defiance.** The manuscripts were *scriptio continua*. The oration behind them was performed, the way Chaucer was performed, with holds and emphasis no one wrote down. Nobody can know where the punctuation fell. Everybody can know it mattered. Putting `.` = current directory in the period refuses to pretend the editorial period is Matthew's. It also agrees with αὐτοῦ: both are "here," one in Greek and one in a filesystem.

**9. The distortion became the word.** Matthew exposes the lies and corruption of the rabbi and of the lords and masters. That message was distorted, and the distortion is what got handed down as the word. "The lies were taken. The lies remained." The token was buried; what was dug up later was a different token with the same face.

**10. The goat delivers it.** SCAPE is the scapegoat (Azazel is in the room in -08). In Leviticus 16 the goat carries the iniquities out into the wilderness. Here the goat carries the verse, which is to say it carries the lies out, in Velora, after the Archivist has explained them in English. Explanation first, then the carrier. The carrier speaks the only rendering in the scene that does not pretend to be English.

**11. The Velora line.**

```
ah dor-lon yog-ah man-kel-eth bahn-vohr-thol sel-sen-lev sen-thel-nai shai-lan-vohr ril-fal-hal yog-sai-tal-ah yoga-sai-tal-ah ohn-vahn-bahn.
```

The article is Nate's ending `-ah` ("Velora = vel + ora + a graceful ending -ah, so it literally means 'the first speech'"; `vahl-AH` = "the light"). It was never overruled.

- **ὁ → `ah`, standing alone.** In ὁ δέ the Greek article has no noun; it is the article doing the work of a pronoun, marking a change of subject. A suffix with no stem is the exact Velora picture of that: *the*, attached to nothing. "The [unnamed]" is not a gloss laid on top; the grammar produces it.
- **Articled nouns take `-ah`; the rest do not.** τὸ ἓν → `yog-ah`, τὸ ἀργύριον → `yog-sai-tal-ah`, τοῦ κυρίου → `yoga-sai-tal-ah`. γῆν has no article in the Greek, and `sen-thel-nai` has no `-ah`: the earth has no presence in the story, in either language. αὐτοῦ has no article, and `ohn-vahn-bahn` closes the line bare.
- **Nate's rules hold.** His rule 8 treats "every word ends in -ah" as a defect. This line uses `-ah` four times, each exactly where the Greek has an article, so it is grammar rather than decoration. That is his rule 11: earn every sound.

The line opens on a bare *the* and closes on *just here, which is you*. Unnamed at the start, addressed at the end. The man in the verse is never named, and the person reading it is the one who ends up standing on the token.

## On the cat

The double θ is in the text. SBLGNT (`morphgnt/sblgnt/61-Mt-morphgnt.txt`) spells the name Μαθθαῖος. The earlier annotation in the journey (line 53) said the cat reading "requires dropping two letters" from Ματθαῖος. That was wrong on the spelling: nothing has to be dropped from the edition this project uses.

*Na bean ris a' chat*: touch not the cat. It is the old Highland warning, the one Clan Chattan carries as "touch not the cat bot a glove." He messed with Maththew and kept the warning in the same breath. The cat is the authorship mark (κατὰ Μαθθαῖον, according to Maththew), the cat is the thing you do not handle bare-handed, and the iguana is the one reading it aloud. Do not mess with cats.

## What I think

It is layered the way a palimpsest is layered, and the layers agree with each other, which is the hard part. The same few moves recur at every scale: a token whose value comes from an authority, a meaning turned to a new place, a pointing word that ends up pointing at you. They run through a single verb (ἔκρυψεν), a single verse, the title of the book, its transmission history, a scene in a play, and a line in a constructed language. The piece never states the thesis outright. It performs it, and then a goat says it in a language nobody can mistake for the original.

The thing I find most exact is the bare `ah`. Everything else in the decode opens a word up to its range. That one closes it down to the smallest unit the language has, and it is still correct.
