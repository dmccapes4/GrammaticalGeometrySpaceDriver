## I. The room and the cadence

```
narrator  ·  question ;  answer .
```

The `·` comes right after *λέγων*, *εἶπεν*, *λέγει*. "He said·". It's a colon. It means *evaluate what follows*. The `;` closes a question. The `.` closes an answer.

**MCCLANE:** That's a terminal. Prompt, input, output.

**FEYNMAN:** That's a REPL. Read, evaluate, print, loop. The lecture is a REPL session.

**MCCLANE:** How many questions are in this guy's lecture compared to the poetry guy?

**FEYNMAN:** Careful. Last pass compared per verse against per line. A verse is not a line. Normalize per word. Maththew: 18,329 words, `;` at **9.17 per thousand**. Homer: 199,075 words, **2.31 per thousand**. Four times. Four times the gates.

## X. 17:20 — the result

> ὁ δὲ ⸀λέγει αὐτοῖς· Διὰ τὴν ⸀ὀλιγοπιστίαν ὑμῶν· ἀμὴν γὰρ λέγω ὑμῖν, ἐὰν ἔχητε πίστιν ὡς κόκκον σινάπεως, ἐρεῖτε τῷ ὄρει τούτῳ· ⸂Μετάβα ἔνθεν⸃ ἐκεῖ, καὶ μεταβήσεται, καὶ οὐδὲν ἀδυνατήσει ⸀ὑμῖν.

**FEYNMAN:** This is the answer to the whole section, and it's a beauty. Two words to set side by side.

```
ὀλιγοπιστία        oligo-pistia        "little-faith"     a small amount of faith
κόκκος σινάπεως    a mustard seed                         the smallest seed they had
```

**MCCLANE:** Both are small.

**FEYNMAN:** Both are small. So it's not about size. If it were about size he'd say "you need more." He says the opposite. He says a mustard seed is enough. The smallest unit they knew.

**MCCLANE:** Then what's the difference?

**FEYNMAN:** *ὀλιγο-* is a fraction. "A little of." Part of a whole. It's faith written as a ratio. Some faith over total possible faith.

A seed is a **whole**. A mustard seed isn't a little bit of a seed. It's one seed. A complete count.

**MCCLANE:** Thalkethen.

**FEYNMAN:** Section III.2, word for word: *"A partial is not a new kind of number. It is a whole count at a finer yog that the observer did not declare."* The students have faith as a partial. He tells them: declare a smaller yog. Make it one seed. One whole count of faith at the mustard-seed unit. Then it's not a fraction anymore. It's a surplus. Nonzero S.

**MCCLANE:** And with nonzero S—

**FEYNMAN:**

```
J  =  S  ×  Π  ×  (yog into τ)
S = one seed, whole, nonzero
Π = the potential they were missing (δύναμις)
τ = a mountain's worth of attenuation
```

S is tiny, τ is huge, J is small but it isn't zero. Integrate J over time and the mountain moves. *Μετάβα ἔνθεν ἐκεῖ.* "Move from here to there." A displacement vector. *καὶ μεταβήσεται*: and it will move.

**MCCLANE:** And the last line.

**FEYNMAN:** *οὐδὲν ἀδυνατήσει ὑμῖν.* "Nothing will be im-possible to you." *ἀ-δυνατ-*: no-δύναμις. The same root as 17:19. They asked "why didn't we have the potential," and the last word of the answer is: with a whole seed, there's no case where the potential is absent.

**MCCLANE:** Question in 17:19 uses the root. Answer in 17:20 closes on the root.

**FEYNMAN:** The gate opened and closed on the same variable. That's how you know it's a lecture. Somebody built that.
